Identification rate of comet and guest-star articles

Share of comet-keyword article dates that fall within ±90 days of a known comet's perihelion (JPL Small-Body Database, 376 comets with perihelion before 1911), against the share for the same number of random dates (1,000 draws).

78%of 69 Sillok reports within ±90 d of a perihelion (chance 17%)
Key point

Matching comet and guest-star report dates to known perihelia within ±90 days captures 78% of Sillok observation reports and 33% of Goryeosa articles, against 17% and 3% for the same number of random dates. The chance rate is the share of a source's span covered by ±90-day windows around known perihelia, so it differs by period; for the Ilgi (1623–1910) it reaches 33%, so read it with the sample precision (0.39).

Formula
Rate = articles inside the window / dated articles. The ±90-day window was fixed before computing and not adjusted. Control: random dates drawn from the source's span, as many as articles, 1,000 times (seed 20261102).
How to read it
The excess over the chance rate is the information. Sillok observation reports (the final 'yes' of the 1.0 precision sample — 64 coder yes plus 5 editorial overrides, 69 in all; up to 1.2.1 only the 64 were used), all keyword articles, the Goryeosa treatise and the Ilgi are shown side by side. The chance rate is the share of the source's span covered by ±90-day windows around known perihelia, so it rises for later periods with more known comets.
Caveats
Most historical orbits in the JPL list were themselves computed from Chinese records, so the check is not fully independent. Ilgi comet-keyword precision is 0.39 on a 107-article sample — 妖星 and 彗孛 are often omen rhetoric.
