Solar-eclipse articles matched by computation

Articles mentioning '日食' are typed (observation report, forecast, rescue ritual, non-occurrence, discussion); the metric is the share whose date lies within ±1 day of a computed eclipse visible from Seoul.

65.5%of 84 observation reports match a Seoul eclipse
Key point

Answers 'can the Sillok's eclipse records be trusted' by type: 65% of observation reports match an eclipse visible from Seoul and 95% an eclipse somewhere on Earth; most of the rest are post-sunset eclipses entered as computed. Counting every '日食' article as an observation drops the rate to 24%, so never use the untyped total.

Formula
per type: matched / articles of that type. Eclipses computed from DE406 at every new moon, testing whether Sun and Moon overlap above the Seoul horizon. The ±1-day window matches the calendar conversion uncertainty; the ±0 value is given too.
How to read it
The match rate of observation reports is the site's headline number. Forecast and ritual articles include eclipses that were never visible (below the horizon or elsewhere), so lower rates there are expected.
Caveats
Types were hand-coded (codebook published) with precision and CI on the reliability card. Ahn & Lee (2004) counted events; we count articles, so the totals differ.
External check
Ahn & Lee (2004) Table 1: 231 Sillok eclipse records, 11 mis-dated, 60 not visible.
Reliability card
externalExternal match — compared with an astronomical computation

Of 84 observation-report articles, 55 fall within ±1 day of a computed eclipse visible from Seoul = 65.5% (Wilson 95% 54.8%–74.8%); 95.2% (80) match an eclipse somewhere on Earth (Espenak–Meeus canon).

Typing: all 332 articles were coded by four coders; 34 were blind re-coded by a fifth — agreement 91.2%, κ = 0.89 (κ = 0.94 when pooled into observation / not an eclipse / other).
